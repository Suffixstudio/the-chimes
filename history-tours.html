Our History

A bath house, a hotel, an antique store, and now this.

Lory and Wilma Watts renamed it The Chimes in 1972 and ran an antique store here for thirty years. The painted sign is still on the wall. Every era left something behind in these redwood walls, and that is what we are preserving as the building opens its next chapter.

1887 · The Crescent Bath House

A famous mineral-springs spa

Designed by Frank Ferris and funded by city founder Franklin Heald, the Moorish-style bath house offered ten sunken porcelain tubs, mud baths, and a mineral plunge, all fed by a hot sulfur spring that surfaced at 132°F. Guests traveled from across the country to soak in its waters, prized as a remedy for arthritis, rheumatism, and the aches of the age.

Early 1900s · The Resort Era

The social heart of town

With its sunken plunge pool and grand rooms, the bath house became a gathering place for social events and musical entertainment in the young resort city of Elsinore.

Mid-Century · Many Lives

A landmark that adapted

As the town changed, so did the building. It served as a rooming house and then an interior-design studio, while its one-of-a-kind façade kept watch over downtown.

1972–2002 · “The Chimes”

The name that stuck

Owners Lory and Wilma Watts renamed it The Chimes and ran a beloved antique shop here for three decades. In 1975 the building was listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Today · The Next Chapter

Restored for what's next

Preserved on the outside and thoughtfully modernized within, The Chimes opens its doors again as an event venue, a workspace, and a living landmark.

By appointment

Tours run by appointment only, Friday through Sunday from 9am to 6pm, with groups of up to 10 so the home keeps its intimate feel. Adults are $7, children 5 to 12 are $4, children under 5 are free, and a visit lasts about 30 minutes. As a privately owned historic property, your entrance fee directly supports the preservation and restoration of the building. And before you go, stop by the gift shop inside and take a piece of the story home.

On the tour

Rooms most people never get to see.

The tour goes up the main staircase and through the house, room by room, left much as it was. Restoration is still underway, so the route shifts as work moves through the building. No two tours are quite the same.

Preservation

The Crescent House Preservation Project.

A landmark this old is never truly finished. The Crescent House Preservation Project is the ongoing work of restoring and maintaining the building, run by The Crescent Bathhouse Foundation. Alongside it, the Borsellinos are building a cultural center across the street, an extension of The Crescent House that will host community events and add venue space for The Chimes.

The Crescent Bathhouse Foundation, Inc. is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization, EIN 41-4891212. Your donation is tax deductible to the extent allowed by law and directly supports the restoration and preservation of this historic 1887 landmark for the Lake Elsinore community.
