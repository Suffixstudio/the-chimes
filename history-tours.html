Our History

The most storied address in the valley.

Each era left its mark on these redwood walls, and that history is exactly what we protect as the building opens its next chapter.

1887

A famous mineral-springs spa

Designed by Frank Ferris and funded by city founder Franklin Heald, the Moorish-style bath house offered ten sunken porcelain tubs, mud baths, and a mineral plunge, all fed by a hot sulfur spring that surfaced at 132°F. Guests travelled from across the country to soak in its waters, prized as a remedy for arthritis, rheumatism, and the aches of the age.

Early 1900s

The social heart of town

With its sunken plunge pool and grand halls, the bath house became a gathering place for social events and musical entertainment in the young resort city of Elsinore.

Mid-century

A landmark that adapted

As the town changed, so did the building. It served as a rooming house and then an interior-design studio, while its one-of-a-kind façade kept watch over downtown.

1972 to 2002

The name that stuck

Owners Lory and Wilma Watts renamed it The Chimes and ran a beloved antique shop here for three decades. In 1975 the building was listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Today

Restored for what's next

Preserved on the outside and thoughtfully modernised within, The Chimes reopens as a home for gatherings, workspace, and celebrations, ready to write its next hundred years.

From the archive

The building people travelled to see.

In its first decades, people came from across the country for the mineral springs. The Crescent Bath House was not a local curiosity, it was a destination, and postcards of it were carried home across the state.

The façade you see on Graham Avenue today is the same one they photographed.
