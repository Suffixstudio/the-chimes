It was one of Southern California's celebrated mineral-springs health resorts. Built in 1887 over a natural hot spring, the bath house drew visitors from across the country who came to soak in its 132-degree sulfur waters and mineral baths, long considered a remedy for arthritis, rheumatism, and other ailments. Lake Elsinore was a destination town in those years and the building sat at the center of it. That same landmark is The Crescent House today, with The Chimes as its event venue, and the history tour walks the parts that are open as restoration continues.

It is. It went up in 1887 as the Crescent Bath House, and that is the name it carries on the National Register, reference number 75000453, added July 30, 1975. That status is part of why the restoration moves the way it does, room by room, keeping original woodwork, stained glass, and detail rather than replacing it. It also shapes practical things here: the Graham Avenue entrance has steps, there is no elevator to the second floor, and some outside rentals need their own power source. Ask early and we will tell you plainly what the building allows.

It has a reputation. Decades of visitors have shared ghost stories tied to the old bathhouse, and it's long been considered one of the most storied buildings in the valley. We'll let you decide.
